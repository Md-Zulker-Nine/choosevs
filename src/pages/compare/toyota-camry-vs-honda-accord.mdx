## Overview

The Toyota Camry and Honda Accord remain the benchmark options in the midsize sedan class. For decades, car shoppers have narrowed their choice down to these two family haulers because both deliver predictable reliability, comfortable cabins, and strong resale value.

Toyota overhauled the Camry into an all-hybrid lineup, combining a 2.5-liter engine with electric power as standard equipment while adding optional all-wheel drive across all grades. Honda takes a split approach with the Accord, offering an affordable 1.5-liter turbocharged base engine alongside an optional 2.0-liter hybrid powertrain on upper trims. Choosing between them depends on whether you prioritize standard fuel economy and all-weather traction or maximum cargo space and lower initial purchase pricing.

## Toyota Camry at a Glance

The Toyota Camry focuses entirely on electrified efficiency and user-friendly practical features. By standardizing its hybrid powertrain, every driver benefits from 225 combined horsepower and an impressive fuel economy rating of 53 mpg city and 50 mpg highway.

Key highlights include:
- Standard hybrid engine setup delivering instantaneous electric torque
- Electronic On-Demand All-Wheel Drive option for enhanced cold-weather traction
- Outstanding total range of approximately 640 miles on a full gas tank
- Quick 0-60 mph sprint of 7.4 seconds

## Honda Accord at a Glance

The Honda Accord maintains its reputation for sleek styling and precise steering response. Its base trim features a responsive 1.5-liter turbocharged four-cylinder engine generating 192 horsepower and 192 lb-ft of torque at a competitive entry price point of $28,295.

Key highlights include:
- Class-leading trunk capacity measuring 16.7 cubic feet
- Strong low-end torque from the standard 1.5L turbocharged engine
- Available 204-horsepower hybrid setup on higher-tier trim levels
- Refined interior ergonomics with straightforward physical switchgear

## Head-to-Head

### Powertrain and Efficiency
Toyota fundamentally altered the baseline by equipping every Camry with a 2.5-liter hybrid system. Output stands at 225 horsepower in front-wheel drive configuration, yielding 53 mpg city and 50 mpg highway. The vehicle accelerates from zero to 60 mph in 7.4 seconds, giving it crisp responsiveness away from traffic lights.

The entry-level Honda Accord relies on a standard 1.5-liter turbocharged engine making 192 horsepower. Fuel economy for this base trim comes in at 29 mpg city and 37 mpg highway. Honda offers a 204-horsepower hybrid system on upper trims, achieving 48 mpg city and 47 mpg highway, but it requires spending more money upfront. For baseline efficiency, the Camry holds a clear advantage.

### All-Weather Capability
If you live in snowy or wet climates, drivetrain choices matter significantly. Toyota offers an Electronic On-Demand All-Wheel Drive system across all Camry trims by adding a dedicated electric motor to the rear axle. This increases total output to 232 horsepower while providing instant rear-wheel traction when slippage occurs.

The Honda Accord remains strictly front-wheel drive across every trim level, including its hybrid variants. Drivers who regularly navigate winter storms will find the Camry far better suited to severe weather.

### Interior Utility and Cargo
Inside the cabin, both vehicles seat five passengers comfortably, but trunk capacity differs. The Accord delivers 16.7 cubic feet of rear cargo space, outclassing the Camry's 15.1 cubic feet. The extra volume in the Accord makes fitting large suitcases or bulky strollers easier.

Both dashboards feature clean layouts with tactile knobs for volume and climate controls. However, the Accord's slightly airier cabin design provides a marginally open feel for front and rear passengers alike.

### Pricing and Total Cost of Ownership
The Honda Accord undercuts the Camry's base price with a starting MSRP of $28,295 against the Camry's $28,700 starting point. However, that $405 savings on the Accord comes with a traditional gasoline engine returning lower fuel economy. The Camry's standard hybrid setup quickly closes that price gap through gas savings, especially for commuters covering high annual mileage.

## Who Should Buy Which

**Buy the Toyota Camry if:**
- You want maximum fuel efficiency without upgrading to expensive upper trims.
- You live in a snow-belt region and require all-wheel drive capability.
- You value extended highway range, allowing up to 640 miles between fuel stops.

**Buy the Honda Accord if:**
- You want the lowest possible initial entry price in a full-size midsize car.
- You require maximum trunk capacity for gear, luggage, or equipment.
- You prefer the linear punch of a low-end turbocharged engine over a CVT hybrid response.

## Pros and Cons

### Toyota Camry

**Pros**

- Standard hybrid powertrain yields up to 53 mpg city
- Available Electronic On-Demand All-Wheel Drive across all trims
- Quicker acceleration at 7.4 seconds from 0 to 60 mph
- Impressive total driving range of approximately 640 miles

**Cons**

- Slightly higher starting price of $28,700
- Smaller cargo volume at 15.1 cubic feet
- Engine noise under heavy acceleration

**Best for:** Commuters and suburban drivers prioritizing maximum fuel efficiency, year-round AWD confidence, and low long-term running costs.

### Honda Accord

**Pros**

- Lower entry price starting at $28,295
- Generous cargo volume at 16.7 cubic feet
- Higher base engine torque at 192 lb-ft
- Sleek profile with intuitive infotainment controls

**Cons**

- Base trim relies on standard gas engine rather than hybrid
- No option for all-wheel drive on any trim level
- Slower 0-60 mph time at 7.9 seconds

**Best for:** Drivers looking for a spacious trunk, engaging chassis dynamics, and a lower initial purchase price without needing all-wheel drive.

## The Verdict

**Winner: Toyota Camry**

The Toyota Camry claims the top spot thanks to its standard hybrid powertrain across all models and the availability of all-wheel drive. While the Honda Accord offers slightly better cargo room and lower base pricing, the Camry delivers superior fuel efficiency, quicker acceleration, and longer total range.

Choose the Toyota Camry if you want standard hybrid efficiency, available all-wheel drive for winter weather, and maximum range per tank. Choose the Honda Accord if you want a lower entry price, a larger trunk, or prefer a non-hybrid base powertrain with crisp low-end torque.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does the Toyota Camry come standard as a hybrid?

Yes, for the 2025 model year, Toyota made the Camry exclusively a hybrid, pairing a 2.5-liter four-cylinder engine with electric motors for 225 horsepower in front-wheel drive.

### Which sedan has all-wheel drive available?

The Toyota Camry offers optional Electronic On-Demand All-Wheel Drive across all trim levels. The Honda Accord is strictly front-wheel drive.

### Which vehicle offers better fuel economy?

The Toyota Camry achieves up to 53 mpg city and 50 mpg highway. The base Honda Accord gets 29/37 mpg, while the Accord Hybrid reaches 48 mpg city and 47 mpg highway.

### Which sedan has a larger trunk?

The Honda Accord offers 16.7 cubic feet of trunk space, compared to 15.1 cubic feet in the Toyota Camry.
